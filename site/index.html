Four hours of retries, then the orders stop arriving.

When a store’s webhook receiver starts returning errors, Shopify retries the delivery up to 8 times over 4 hours. If it is still failing, Shopify removes the webhook subscription. New orders stop reaching the ERP or warehouse system, and the storefront looks completely normal the whole time.

Cronus raises the alarm within minutes of the first confirmed failure, while the retries are still running and the subscription can still be saved.

Source: Shopify developer docs, “Troubleshoot webhooks”.

What Cronus does, exactly

Four mechanics, no black box. These are the same rules the engine runs on.

Two-failure verification

A failed check is repeated 20 seconds later. Only a second failure opens an incident, so a single dropped packet or a slow deploy restart doesn’t page anyone. While an incident stays open, duplicate alerts are held back.

Questions agencies ask

Does Cronus need access to our codebase or servers? +

No. Cronus sends ordinary HTTP requests to the URLs you give it, from outside your infrastructure, the same way a customer or Shopify would. There’s nothing to install and no credentials to share.

How does the 20-second re-check prevent false alarms? +

Most one-off failures are a blip: a dropped connection, a container restarting during a deploy. When a check fails, Cronus waits 20 seconds and checks again. Only if the second check also fails does it open an incident and send the alert. That cuts out most false alarms; it can’t promise none, because an endpoint that is down for exactly those 20 seconds will still be reported.
